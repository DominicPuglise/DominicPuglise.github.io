Rack Mounted Variac
A major incentive for this project was test the structural rigidity of a simple rack-mounted faceplate under torsional loading. The power supply itself is a relatively simple 3-Amp variable autotransformer matched with a 1:1 isolation transformer. The unit serves as a safe and dependable means of powering a variety of AC devices, but can also be paired with a simple rectifier extension for a rudimentary DC supply.

This project was largely intended to be a proof of concept as a cost-effective means of creating rack-mountable equipment. The faceplate itself consists of 16-gauge steel cut down to size, with two 1.5"x15" steel angles welded to the backside. A stress analysis of the mechanical design was first performed through ANSYS, with a shearing load of 40 pounds distributed across the steel angles, and a torsional load of 10 pound-foot was applied to the face to account for transformer overhang. The amount of true deformation on the faceplate was shown to be smaller than 1 mm.

The end product agreed with the computer simulation and provided extremely satisfactory results, while remaining quite cheap (total cost of metal being under $20). This validates a crucial test of concept and will allow for further homemade rack-mountable equipment pieces in the future, as well as an incremental step towards more complex self-fabricated rack-mountable equipment.
